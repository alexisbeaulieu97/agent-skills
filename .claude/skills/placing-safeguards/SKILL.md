# Placing safeguards

A rule protects the work only where it holds when nobody is thinking about it.
Whoever does the work next, a person or an agent, sees only what is in front of
them, copies the nearest example and takes the shortest path that seems to
work. So put each safeguard where it holds for that reader: the stronger the
place, the less it depends on anyone noticing, remembering or agreeing.

## The layers, strongest first

- **The work itself.** Change the shape of the work so the mistake cannot be
  made, or so the right way is the easiest and the one there is to copy: one
  place for each fact instead of copies kept in step, a choice removed when it
  has one sensible answer, the old way taken away rather than discouraged. As
  illustrations: a total computed by a formula rather than typed in, a door
  that closes and locks on its own, a data type that cannot hold an invalid
  value. Once done, the problem is gone for every later case at no further
  cost.
- **An automatic check.** Where the mistake can still be made, have something
  catch it every time, before it lands, without anyone remembering to look: a
  form that refuses an incomplete answer, a test that fails, a scale that
  rejects an overweight bag at check-in. A check's message says what to do
  instead, so the one who meets it can fix it on the spot.
- **Written guidance.** Instructions, notes, rules and checklists hold only
  where they are read, understood and applied, and they cost attention every
  time. Keep them for what truly needs judgment, and keep them few: each one
  added dilutes the others. Guidance is read where the reader meets the
  decision, so put it there: in a check's message that names the rule and its
  fix, in a field of the form or template being filled in, in a note linked
  from where the work happens. A separate document nobody opens at that
  moment goes unread and drifts out of date.
- **A reviewer's judgment.** Someone who did not do the work reads it against
  the guidance no check enforces. It is the only layer that catches what
  nobody foresaw, and less reliable than a check. It takes two forms. A fresh
  agent is cheap enough to keep up with the volume of work. A person is the
  scarcest form: attention fades with repetition and does not keep up with
  volume, so spend it where judgment matters most and on what an agent's
  review misses, rather than on what a stronger layer could catch.

## Place each safeguard

Look past the instance to the kind of mistake: the same wrong turn taken
twice, or the same correction given twice, marks a kind that will recur.

Start at the strongest layer and go down only when a higher one cannot hold
the rule, for a reason you can name: it would block legitimate cases, it
costs more than the mistake does, or the rule needs judgment no check can
make. A rule often splits: the exact part goes to a check, and only the part
that needs judgment stays in guidance or review. Even a rule that needs
judgment can have its occurrence checked: whether the step happened goes to
a check, whether it was done well stays with the reviewer. As illustrations,
a submission refused while its review section is empty, or an expense claim
refused without a receipt attached.

When a stronger layer takes over a rule, guidance that only repeats it goes:
it is noise beside the check, and it dilutes the guidance that still matters.
Guidance written a second time for the same mistake is a sign the rule
belongs higher.

Weigh the safeguard against the mistake: how often it happens, what it costs,
whether it can be undone. A mistake that cannot be undone earns a strong
safeguard from the start; a rare, cheap and reversible one may need none.
A safeguard that gets in the way of legitimate work gets worked around, so
where exceptions are real, leave a visible way to make one, with its reason.

Building a safeguard that changes something shared, and changing what needs
someone's review or approval, fall under whatever rules govern those changes.

## Prove it holds

A safeguard counts once it has been seen to catch the real mistake and to let
correct work through: replay the case that slipped through, and a correct one
beside it. A check that could not have failed proves nothing.

## What a placement is made of

The kind of mistake and the evidence it recurs, the layer chosen and why each
stronger layer could not hold it, and what showed the safeguard catches it.
